## How sensitive is the strategy to its settings?

The Yau-Weinstein stock portfolio returned 11.6% annualized from January 2000 through August 2026, with a 36.5% maximum drawdown. This comparison leaves idle balances in cash. The analysis deducts estimated costs of 0.2% of each purchase and sale from the return on the fill date.

| Change from the baseline | Annualized return | Maximum drawdown |
|---|---:|---:|
| Baseline, 10 positions and daily decisions | 11.6% | 36.5% |
| Stop-loss at 8% / 12% | 11.1% / 11.7% | 32.1% / 35.5% |
| Breakout lookback of 40 / 60 sessions | 11.2% / 11.1% | 36.6% / 32.1% |
| Minimum volume of 1.25× / 1.75× average | 11.1% / 10.9% | 33.5% / 32.3% |
| Maximum of 15 / 20 positions | 9.7% / 9.2% | 30.3% / 28.5% |
| Check the 150-day moving-average exit weekly | 12.2% | 33.4% |
| Check both that exit and upgrades weekly | 9.8% | 40.8% |

The six nearby stop, breakout and volume settings produced broadly similar full-period returns. The stricter volume threshold was weaker over 2016–2025, returning 7.2% against 9.3% for the baseline. The grid did not vary the market filter length.

Doubling the position cap reduced the worst decline by eight percentage points and cost 2.4 points of annualized return. A weekly moving-average exit was promising, but through December 2025 its annualized advantage was only 0.2 points. Checking upgrades weekly as well took closed trades from 1,116 to 705 and deepened the worst decline. These in-sample results favour keeping daily upgrade decisions.
