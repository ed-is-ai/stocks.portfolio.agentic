Key what each one means
629clean

Enough history for both buying and selling, no missing days, every scan reading present.

Only these can produce a Buy. A Hold here is the strategy’s opinion, not a data problem. The FX ceiling on the Strategy data tab still applies.

27thin

Enough data, not enough history. Buying needs more of it than selling, so a stock can pass one and fail the other.

It drops out of whichever side it fails and the recommendations screen will not say so. A holding too short to buy can still be long enough to sell, which is why this table splits the two.

22gapped

Enough trading days on paper, but some are missing out of the middle.

The dangerous one. A 200-day average missing 8 days really reaches back 208. It still calculates, so Stage and VCP come out wrong with no error anywhere.

31missing fragment

Price history is fine, something else is not. Usually a VCP or Stage reading, sometimes volume on a fund.

More history will not help. The strategy asked for it, the scan never produced it.

12dropped

Never got in at all. Wrong day’s data, an ambiguous ticker, two tickers colliding, or no prices.

Recommendations cannot see these. Hold one and it is forced to Hold; do not and it never comes up. These 12 are the losses we can explain, which is what the count at the top is for.

The baseline. Anything with data passes. If a Buy and Hold comparison looks odd, it is the logic and not the data.

No new storage. Sessions, columns and readings from evidence_coverage. Drop reasons from unresolved. Expected trading days from the pinned calendar. The FX ceiling from fx:GBPUSD=X. Thresholds from each strategy’s evidence_requirements. Discovery totals from SourceHealth.count.

Two kinds of requirement. A strategy can ask for four things per stock: price history and the three scan readings, with separate minimums for buying and selling. FX, the calendar and the benchmark are not on that list; they are fixed per run. So a strategy can be stopped by something it has no way to ask for.
